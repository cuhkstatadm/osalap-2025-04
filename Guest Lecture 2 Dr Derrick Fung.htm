01 甚麼是精算學？

我們首先來探討一下甚麼是精算學（Actuarial Science），以及精算師（Actuary）到底是做甚麼的。還記得我讀大學時，親戚朋友問我修讀甚麼專業，當我回答「精算」時，他們的第一反應往往是：「精算就是計數嗎？」即便到現在，當我面試透過 JUPAS（大學聯合招生辦法） 報讀的同學，問他們為何想讀精算時，最常聽到的答案依然是：「因為我喜歡數學和計數，所以想做精算師。」

然而，精算真的僅僅是計算嗎？如果只是純粹的計算，那它與統計師或數據分析員（Data Analyst）又有何分別？其實，精算學最核心且獨特的地方在於量化不確定性（Quantifying Uncertainty）。當親戚問我精算是做甚麼的，我會用一句最簡單的話來概括：「其實我們最主要的工作，就是計算你何時『離世』。」這個說法雖然直接，卻能讓人立刻明白精算的本質：精算師必須預測未來人們發生各種重大事件的概率（Probability），包括身故、入院、罹患重疾或遇上意外，並計算這些事件所帶來的財務影響（Financial Impact）。

大家不妨將精算師想像成一位「天氣預測員」。天文台的職責是預測變幻莫測的天氣，而精算師的職責則是預測人生中各種充滿不確定性（Uncertainty）的大事。當你遇上不幸，必然會面臨財務損失：身故會令家人失去經濟支柱；生病入院需要支付高昂的醫療費用；受傷停工會導致收入中斷。精算師就是透過收集龐大的統計與財務數據，結合經濟理論與商業決策，來釐定能夠覆蓋這些財務損失（Financial Losses）的合適保費。

提起天文台，大家可能會笑說天文台的預測經常不準確——總是在放學後才懸掛風球，或者在上學途中突然傾盆大雨。其實，這正點出了精算與天氣預測的最大共通點：對未來的預測永遠無法達到 100% 的絕對準確。精算模型或許預測某個群體的平均壽命是 80 歲，但具體到個人，可能 79 歲或 82 歲離世，無人能精確到某年某月某日。既然無法做到絕對準確，精算師能做的就是盡量收窄預測的誤差（Margin of Error）。就如大家在數學課學過的標準差（Standard Deviation），精算師的目標是將實際理賠結果與預測值之間的標準差縮到最小，以此來嚴謹地控制和管理估算時所涉及的風險與不確定性。

02 精算師的工作範疇

如果成功考取資格，精算師可以在哪些領域大展拳腳？我的第一份工作是在中銀人壽（BOC Life）擔任精算工作，這屬於人壽及健康保險（Life and Health Insurance）的範疇。這類業務涵蓋了身故賠償、住院醫療、危疾保障以及長期儲蓄保險等。此外，當政府推行大型醫療改革時，往往也需要聘請精算顧問來評估龐大的公共醫療開支。其後，我轉職至保險業監管局（Insurance Authority, IA），這便涉及了監管與合規（Regulatory and Compliance）領域。

為何監管機構極度需要精算專業人才？因為保監局負責監管各大保險公司的營運，而保險公司提交的財務報表（Financial Statements）背後全都是精算數據。假設一間保險公司申報其未來的負債（Liabilities）高達一百億，監管機構如何判斷這個數字是高估還是低估？雖然有外部審計師負責核數，但如果缺乏精算知識去審視其底層的精算假設，依然容易出現嚴重的財務漏洞。早前就有一間信託公司因審計報告存在不當成分而被公開譴責，可見專業監管的必要性。

除了壽險與監管機構，許多精算師亦活躍於一般保險 / 非人壽保險（General / Non-life Insurance）領域。這包括與生命無直接關聯的產品，例如旅遊保險（涵蓋行李遺失、航班延誤）、汽車保險（Motor Insurance）以及涵蓋火災或財物損失的家居保險。

此外，退休金（Pension）及強積金（MPF）也是精算師的重要戰場。在香港，除了公務員享有長俸（即退休後每月發放退休金直至身故的定期給付制度），大部分私人機構僱員依賴的是一次性提取的強積金。然而，許多跨國企業仍保留著長俸福利。由於人類的壽命不斷延長，加上股市投資的回報波動，要確保這筆長期資金足以應付未來的退休金派發，企業必須依賴精算師來精確計算每月的派發金額、支付期限以及投資風險。精算師在評估投資時，不單看表面的回報率，更著重於風險調整後回報（Risk-adjusted Return）。同時，銀行業及大型顧問公司（Consulting Firms）亦會聘請精算師，協助企業處理牽涉風險資本（Risk Capital）的複雜項目及企業風險管理。

03 核心職責：產品定價與準備金

了解了行業分佈，那麼精算師在保險公司的日常核心工作是甚麼呢？相信大家已具備一些基本概念：保險公司的營運必須維持收支平衡。若保費訂得太高，產品便失去競爭力，客戶會流失；若保費過低，公司則會面臨巨額虧損甚至破產。因此，精算師的兩大核心職責便應運而生：

第一項核心工作是產品定價（Pricing）。精算師必須利用大量的歷史數據，準確推算出受保群體的死亡率（Mortality Rate）、發病率（Morbidity Rate）以及意外發生的機率，從而為每款保險產品釐定出一個既能覆蓋風險成本，又能保持市場競爭力的合理保費率（Premium Rates）。

第二項核心工作是計提準備金 / 儲備金（Reserving）。保險公司收取了客戶的保費後，絕對不能隨意將這筆資金視作當年利潤而揮霍掉。保險是一份對未來的長期承諾。如果客戶連續供款十年，在第十一年發生意外，保險公司必須有充足的流動資金履行理賠（Claims）責任。因此，如果保險公司今天收取了 100 元保費，精算師可能會要求公司將其中的 80 至 90 元嚴格撥入儲備金中，以應對未來不可預見的賠償。準確計算這筆準備金的規模，是保障公司免受流動性危機衝擊的關鍵。

04 案例分析：AIG 與 CDS 危機

提到保險公司的破產與危機，不得不提 2008 年震驚全球金融界的 AIG（美國國際集團） 危機。首先必須釐清一個歷史誤區：當年陷入危機的是母公司 AIG，而不是其子公司 AIA（友邦保險）。當時香港許多 AIA 客户因為分不清兩者的財務獨立性而引起恐慌退保。為穩定市場，我在保監局工作時，第一時間向 AIA 下達了嚴格指令，禁止其將任何資金（包括轉移至母公司）調離香港，確保了本地投保人的資金安全。

那麼，龐大的 AIG 為何會走到破產邊緣？其致命傷並非傳統的人壽保險業務，而是其金融衍生工具部門大量售賣的 信用違約互換（Credit Default Swap, 簡稱 CDS）。

甚麼是 CDS？簡單來說，它就像是為債券買的一份「破產保險」。假設一間企業發行了債券，但投資者擔心該企業會倒閉而不敢購買。此時，該企業（或投資者）可以向 AIG 支付一筆保費，要求 AIG 提供擔保（Guarantee）。AIG 承諾：萬一該企業真的違約破產，AIG 將代為償還債券的本金與利息。有了這層擔保，債券自然大受歡迎。

在經濟繁榮時，AIG 透過售賣 CDS 賺取了海量且看似「零風險」的保費。然而，當次按危機爆發，按揭證券（Mortgage-Backed Securities, MBS）市場崩盤，引發了骨牌效應。底層企業出現了大規模、系統性的違約（Default）。投資者紛紛湧向 AIG 要求履行擔保責任。由於違約是集中且同時發生的，AIG 瞬間面臨無法承受的天價賠償，最終面臨流動性枯竭，必須由美國政府出手注資拯救。這案例深刻說明了，忽視系統性風險（Systemic Risk）會為金融機構帶來何等毀滅性的後果。

05 財務建模與保險產品設計

除了定價和準備金，財務建模（Financial Modeling）和預測（Forecasting）亦是精算師的拿手好戲。所有的精算模型都建立在成千上萬的假設（Assumptions）之上，例如預期壽命、退保率以及未來的利率走勢。假設當前的模型預測年利率為 4%，若未來宏觀環境轉變，利率下跌至 3.5% 或飆升至 4.5%，公司收取的保費還足夠嗎？準備金需要增加嗎？公司的利潤會受到多大衝擊？精算師需要將這數萬個變數組合（Combinations of assumptions）輸入模型中進行嚴謹的壓力測試（Stress Testing），以找出身處最劣境況（Worst-case scenario）時，哪一個風險因子對公司的利潤影響最深。

這種嚴謹的財務推演直接影響到產品設計（Product Design）。我曾經讓一些沒有精算背景的大學生嘗試設計一款保險產品，他們為了吸引眼球，設計出了一款「保證每年提供 7% 回報」的儲蓄產品。聽起來的確極具吸引力，連我都想立刻投保，因為它能輕鬆跑贏通脹。

但在現實的商業世界中，這是極度危險且不切實際的。保險公司向客戶承諾了 7% 的保證回報（Guaranteed Return），那麼公司拿著這筆錢去投資，實際需要賺取多少回報才能回本？答案是至少 9% 到 10% 以上！因為保險公司必須預留 2-3% 作為營運利潤，同時還要支付龐大的行政開支、員工薪金以及前線代理人的佣金（Commission）。要每年穩定賺取 10% 的回報，資金就必須投入極高風險的資產（如波動極大的科技股），但保險公司的首要原則是穩健，絕不能拿客戶的保命錢去豪賭。因此，保險公司的資金多數投資於低風險的債券（Bonds）（回報約 4-6%），最多只會配置極少量的股票。綜合計算下來，保險公司能提供給客戶的合理回報頂多在 4.5% 左右。這就是為何精算師在產品設計中扮演著「把關者」的角色——杜絕不切實際的幻想，確保產品的財務可行性。

06 破產啟示：HIH 與 Equitable Life

為了確保大家保持清醒，我們來做一個簡單的精算問答。假設我現在要為 100 個人承保，根據統計，未來一年預計會有 1 個人不幸跌斷腿，而保險公司需要為此賠償 1 萬元。請問，為了做到收支平衡，保險公司最少需要向這 100 人每人收取多少保費？答案非常直觀：1 萬元除以 100 人，等於每人 100 元的純保費（Pure Premium）。這看似小學程度的數學，但在現實中，如果管理層無視精算師算出的這條底線，災難便會降臨。

澳洲曾經有一間名為 HIH 保險 的巨頭，最終走向了破產。精算師明明算出每人應收 100 元才能覆蓋風險，為何它還是破產了？第一，因為 100 元只是賠償成本，公司還需要支付營運成本（Operational Costs）和代理佣金，實際收取的保費理應更高。第二，管理層為求急速擴張，選擇了極度進取的削價戰略，以低於成本的超平價保費去掠奪市場份額（Market Share），導致準備金嚴重不足。這就像現時許多互聯網外賣平台，明知虧損也要瘋狂派發補貼來搶佔市場。但保險公司的營運容不得這種燒錢模式，一旦沒有母公司持續的注資（Capital Injection），當理賠潮湧至時，破產便是唯一結局。

另一個經典案例是美國的 Equitable Life。在 1990 年代，美國的宏觀經濟（Macroeconomy）處於高息環境，市場利率高達雙位數。當時 Equitable Life 推出了一款保證 7% 年回報的旗艦產品，在當時看來這是一個非常保守且穩健的承諾。然而，產品大賣之後，美國迎來了漫长的降息週期（Rate-cut Cycle），市場利率急挫。當市場上再也找不到低風險且回報高於 7% 的投資工具時，這款產品便成了公司的巨大失血口。最終，公司因無力兌現對保單持有人的承諾，股價暴跌，無奈被大都會人壽（MetLife）收購。

07 職業排名、薪酬與生活平衡

聽了這麼多精算師的工作日常與金融案例，各位在考慮 JUPAS 選科時，或許會把它納入未來的職業選項。當然，父母可能會傳統地建議你做醫生、做律師，或者近年極受歡迎的工程與人工智能（AI）學科。那麼，精算師在百業中究竟排在甚麼位置？根據權威的 U.S. News & World Report 發佈的最佳職業排名，精算師常年位列全美最佳職業前 11 名，而在 STEM（科學、技術、工程及數學）領域中更是高居第 7 位。

評估一份職業的優劣，我們絕不能單看起薪點，更要綜合考量工作時數、精神壓力以及潛在的執業風險。舉例說，建造業扎鐵工人的日薪極高，但代價是極度耗損體力且日曬雨淋；醫生的收入豐厚，社會地位崇高，但他們面臨著攸關生死的巨大精神壓力，工作時間極長，一旦出現醫療失誤，隨時面臨被吊銷執照的風險；律師雖然賺錢能力強，但往往需要日以繼夜地處理案卷，晉升至合夥人後更要背負尋找客源的沉重壓力。

相比之下，精算師的工作模式顯得更具保障。在保險公司內，任何重大的精算數字絕不會只依賴一個人的計算，你的上司會進行嚴格的核對，最終的簽署責任落在首席精算師（Chief Actuary）身上。即使你真的計算出錯，最壞的結果可能只是被解僱，但你辛苦考取的精算師牌照不會因此被輕易吊銷。精算師的起薪點或許不是全港所有行業中最高的（大學畢業後初入職年薪約幾十萬），但當你累積四、五年經驗並考獲所有專業資格（Professional Qualification）後，年薪突破百萬（即月薪八萬以上）是非常普遍的。如果你渴望一份薪酬豐厚、無需日夜顛倒應酬，且能兼顧工作與生活平衡（Work-Life Balance）的專業，精算學絕對是極佳的選擇。

08 晉升階梯、技能與專業考試

成為精算師的道路是一場持久戰。大學四年畢業只是起點，隨後你需要一邊全職工作，一邊利用週末與深夜的時間準備極度艱深的專業考試。如果你中途放棄，無法考取正精算師（Fellowship, FSA）的資格，你的職涯天花板可能就永遠停留在助理經理（Assistant Manager）的級別。因此，你必須對數學和統計抱有極大的熱忱與決心。

此外，大家必須打破「精算師就是躲在電腦後默默計數」的刻板印象。在職涯初期，你的確需要處理大量的數據收集與計算；但隨著職位晉升，你的核心價值將轉移至「跨部門溝通」與「商業決策」。當你提交一份企業風險管理報告時，你必須向管理層解釋甚麼是 99.5% 置信水平（Confidence Level） 下的 風險價值（Value at Risk, VaR）。如果你直接丟出一個深奧的統計分佈圖，沒有精算背景的 CEO 根本無暇理會甚至會感到惱火。你必須鍛鍊出卓越的溝通技巧，懂得運用淺白易懂的外行語言（Layman Language），將複雜的數學模型翻譯成管理層能一聽就懂的商業指標。

在硬技能方面，掌握編程（Programming / Coding）已是現代精算師的不可或缺的能力。即使你的代碼寫得未夠完美，你至少要懂得如何駕馭 AI 工具來輔助你編寫程式。同時，你必須培養敏銳的商業觸覺（Business Sense），從宏觀角度思考如何利用數據為公司創造利潤。

關於考牌途徑，香港的學生主要會報考美國精算師學會（SOA）或英國精算師學會（IFoA）的專業試。建議大家在高中階段（如 DSE）就打好純數與延伸數學（M1/M2）的堅實基礎。進入大學後，香港大學 (HKU)、香港中文大學 (CUHK) 及香港城市大學 (CityU) 均提供頂尖的精算學位，中大的量化金融（QFIN）亦是非常對口的選擇。畢業後，你需先考取副精算師（ASA），再選擇專科（如壽險、投資）進修以獲取正精算師資格，整個旅程大約需時 5 至 10 年。令人欣慰的是，各大保險公司都設有完善的激勵機制，每當你通過一科考試，公司通常會提供加薪及額外的溫習假期（Study Leave）作全面支援。

09 金融挑戰：利率與債券存續期

在講座的尾聲，我們用一個金融小挑戰來融合今天所學的知識。一間保險公司的財務健康狀況，取決於其資產負債表。公司的資產（Assets）減去負債（Liabilities），剩下的就是屬於股東的股本 / 權益（Equity）。保險公司的龐大資金，最主要的投資渠道是債券。那麼，債券的價格是如何計算出來的？

\[ \text{Bond Price} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{Cashflow}_t}{(1+r)^t} \]

簡單來說，我們是將未來每年收取的票息（Coupon）及到期拿回的本金，進行折現（Discounting），折算為今天的價值。現在問題來了：當市場利率（Market Interest Rate, r）下跌時，債券價格（Bond Price）會上升還是下降？

答案是：價格會上升。觀察上述公式，利率 r 位於分母位置。當利率下跌（分母變小），計算出來的債券價格自然會增加。因此，在降息環境下，保險公司持有的債券資產價值會水漲船高。但請注意，與此同時，保險公司未來需要賠付的負債現值同樣會隨之上升。

那麼，公司的整體權益（股本）到底是增加還是減少呢？這完全取決於到底是「資產升得多」還是「負債升得多」。這引入了金融學中一個極其重要的概念——存續期 / 久期（Duration），它衡量的是資產或負債對利率變動的敏感度。如果資產的存續期大於負債的存續期，當利率下跌時，資產升值的幅度將蓋過負債增加的幅度，公司的股本便會增加；反之，若資產敏感度較低，股本便會縮水。

為了徹底控制這種利率波動帶來的系統性風險，精算師在制定投資策略時，其終極目標是實行嚴格的資產負債匹配（Asset-Liability Matching, ALM），即調整投資組合，令資產的存續期完美等於負債的存續期。如此一來，無論未來的市場利率如何大起大落，公司的淨資產都能穩如泰山，不受外圍宏觀環境的干擾。

01 What is Actuarial Science?

Let us begin by exploring what Actuarial Science truly is and what an Actuary actually does. I distinctly remember when I was an undergraduate, my relatives and friends would ask me what major I was pursuing. When I replied, "Actuarial Science," their immediate follow-up question was inevitably, "So, does that mean you just calculate numbers all day?" Even today, when I interview high school students applying through the JUPAS (Joint University Programmes Admissions System) and ask them why they want to become actuaries, the overwhelmingly common response remains: "Because I love mathematics and calculations, so I want to be an actuary."

But is actuarial science merely about crunching numbers? If it were simply about calculations, how would it differ from the role of a statistician or a Data Analyst? What makes actuarial science fundamentally unique is its focus on Quantifying Uncertainty. When people ask me what my profession entails, I often summarize it with a very direct, albeit blunt, sentence: "Our primary job is to calculate when you are going to 'pass away'." While this might sound striking, it immediately cuts to the core of the profession. Actuaries must forecast the Probability of various major, uncertain life events occurring to individuals in the future—such as death, critical illness, hospitalization, or accidental injury—and strictly evaluate the resulting Financial Impact.

You can conceptualize an actuary as a highly specialized "weather forecaster." While a meteorological observatory predicts the unpredictable nature of the weather, an actuary predicts the unpredictable major events of human life. When misfortune strikes, financial loss inevitably follows: a premature death strips a family of its primary breadwinner; a severe illness results in exorbitant medical bills; a crippling injury halts one's ability to work and generate income. An actuary utilizes vast amounts of statistical data, financial records, and economic theories, interwoven with business acumen, to determine the exact premium required to cover these Financial Losses.

When you think of a weather observatory, what is the first thought that comes to mind? Many of you laugh because you feel their predictions are notoriously inaccurate—hoisting a typhoon signal only after school has been dismissed, or predicting clear skies just before a sudden downpour. Interestingly, this highlights the greatest parallel between meteorology and actuarial science: predictions regarding the future can never achieve 100% absolute accuracy. An actuarial model might predict the average lifespan of a specific cohort to be 80 years, but on an individual level, a person might pass away at 79 or 82. No one can pinpoint the exact day. Since absolute precision is impossible, the actuary's ultimate goal is to minimize the Margin of Error. Just as you have learned the concept of Standard Deviation in your mathematics classes, an actuary strives to shrink the standard deviation between the actual claim payouts and the model's predicted values, thereby rigorously managing the risks inherent in such estimations.

02 Work Areas of an Actuary

Once you successfully obtain your actuarial qualification, in what sectors can you apply your expertise? My first job was serving as an actuary at BOC Life, which falls squarely within the Life and Health Insurance sector. This domain covers life insurance policies, medical and critical illness coverage, as well as long-term savings plans. Furthermore, when the government plans to roll out massive public healthcare reforms, they frequently enlist actuarial consultants to forecast the enormous public expenditures involved. Later in my career, I transitioned to the Insurance Authority (IA), entering the realm of Regulatory and Compliance.

Why do regulatory bodies possess such a critical need for actuarial talent? The IA is tasked with overseeing the operations of major insurance companies. The Financial Statements submitted by these corporations are entirely underpinned by complex actuarial data. If an insurer declares its future Liabilities to be HKD 10 billion, how does the regulator determine whether this figure is inflated or dangerously underestimated? While external auditors review the accounts, without profound actuarial knowledge to scrutinize the underlying assumptions, severe financial loopholes can easily be missed. A recent real-world example involved a trust company being publicly reprimanded because of inappropriate elements embedded within its audited reports—highlighting the absolute necessity of rigorous professional oversight.

Beyond life insurance and government regulation, a significant number of actuaries operate in the General / Non-life Insurance sector. This involves products not directly tied to human mortality, such as travel insurance (covering flight delays and lost baggage), Motor Insurance, and home insurance protecting against fire or property damage.

Additionally, the Pension and MPF (Mandatory Provident Fund) sectors are crucial battlegrounds for actuaries. In Hong Kong, excluding civil servants who receive lifelong pensions, most private-sector employees rely on the MPF, which is typically withdrawn as a lump sum upon retirement. However, many multinational corporations still offer defined-benefit pension schemes, guaranteeing retirees a monthly stipend until death. Given increasing life expectancies and volatile stock market returns, corporations must rely on actuaries to precisely calculate monthly payout amounts, durations, and investment risks to ensure these long-term funds do not dry up. When actuaries evaluate investments, they look beyond superficial yields, focusing intensely on the Risk-adjusted Return. Moreover, the banking industry and top-tier Consulting Firms frequently hire actuaries to help enterprises navigate complex projects involving Risk Capital and enterprise risk management.

03 Core Duties: Pricing & Reserving

Having understood the landscape of the industry, what exactly are the core daily responsibilities of an actuary within an insurance firm? You likely already grasp a fundamental concept: an insurance company must maintain financial equilibrium. If premiums are set too high, the products lose market competitiveness and clients leave. If they are set too low, the company will hemorrhage money and face bankruptcy. Therefore, actuaries are indispensable for executing two highly rigorous tasks:

The first core responsibility is Pricing. Actuaries must harness extensive historical data to accurately project the Mortality Rate, Morbidity Rate, and the probability of accidents for the insured demographic. Using this data, they determine a fair and competitive Premium Rate that sufficiently covers the underlying risk costs.

The second, and arguably more critical, responsibility is calculating the Reserving requirement. When an insurance company collects premiums, it absolutely cannot treat that incoming cash as immediate profit to be spent. Insurance is a long-term promise. If a client pays premiums for ten years and files a claim in the eleventh year, the insurer must possess adequate liquidity to fulfill its Claims obligations. Thus, out of every $100 collected in premiums today, the actuary might mandate that $80 to $90 be strictly allocated into a reserve fund to meet unforeseen future payouts. Calculating the exact, optimal size of this reserve is the linchpin that protects the company against sudden liquidity crises.

04 Case Study: AIG & The CDS Crisis

When discussing insurance bankruptcies and financial crises, it is impossible to ignore the 2008 AIG (American International Group) crisis that sent shockwaves through the global financial system. First, we must clear up a common historical misconception: the entity that faced collapse was the parent company, AIG, not its former subsidiary, AIA. At the time, panic spread among AIA policyholders in Hong Kong who, conflating the two financially independent entities, rushed to surrender their policies. To stabilize the market, during my tenure at the Insurance Authority, my immediate directive was to strictly prohibit AIA Hong Kong from transferring any capital (including to its parent company) out of the jurisdiction without our explicit regulatory approval, successfully safeguarding local policyholders' assets.

So, why did a behemoth like AIG teeter on the brink of bankruptcy? The fatal blow did not come from its traditional life insurance reserves, but rather from the financial derivative instruments sold by its financial products division, specifically the Credit Default Swap (CDS).

What exactly is a CDS? In layman's terms, it operates like a "bankruptcy insurance policy" for corporate bonds. Imagine a company issues a bond, but investors are too terrified of a potential default to buy it. The company (or the investors) can pay a premium to AIG, asking AIG to act as a Guarantee. AIG promises that should the underlying company go bankrupt, AIG will step in to repay the bond's principal and interest to the investors. Backed by AIG's stellar credit rating, these bonds suddenly became highly attractive, and AIG raked in massive, seemingly "risk-free" premiums during the economic boom.

However, when the subprime mortgage crisis erupted, the Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS) market collapsed, triggering a devastating domino effect. A massive, systemic wave of corporate Defaults swept through the market. Investors globally rushed to AIG demanding fulfillment of the guarantees. Because these defaults were highly concentrated and systemic, AIG instantaneously faced astronomical, unpayable claims, causing its liquidity to evaporate and forcing a monumental bailout by the US government. This case profoundly illustrates the catastrophic consequences that Systemic Risk can inflict upon even the largest financial institutions if left unmanaged.

05 Financial Modeling & Product Design

Beyond basic pricing and reserving, Financial Modeling and Forecasting showcase the true analytical prowess of an actuary. Every actuarial model is built upon tens of thousands of Assumptions—such as life expectancy, policy surrender rates, and future interest rate trajectories. If a model currently assumes a 4% annual interest rate, what happens if the macroeconomic environment shifts, causing rates to drop to 3.5% or spike to 4.5%? Will the collected premiums still be sufficient? Will the reserves need drastic adjustment? How severely will the company's profitability be impacted? Actuaries must input these myriad combinations of assumptions into their models to conduct rigorous Stress Testing. This allows them to identify the worst-case scenario and pinpoint which risk factors wield the most significant influence over the company's bottom line.

This rigorous financial forecasting directly governs Product Design. I once tasked a group of university students without actuarial backgrounds to design an insurance product. To make it "attractive," they designed a savings plan boasting a Guaranteed Return of 7% per annum. It sounded phenomenal; even I would be tempted to buy it because a guaranteed 7% effortlessly outpaces inflation.

However, in the real commercial world, launching such a product is borderline suicidal. If an insurance company legally promises a 7% guaranteed return to its clients, what actual yield must the company generate from its investments to break even? The answer is at least 9% to 10%! The company must retain a 2-3% profit margin to cover massive operational overheads, employee salaries, and the hefty Commissions paid to frontline agents. To consistently generate a 10% annual return, the capital would have to be heavily invested in high-risk assets, such as highly volatile tech stocks. But the foundational principle of an insurance company is stability; it absolutely cannot gamble with policyholders' life savings. Consequently, insurers predominantly allocate capital into low-risk Bonds (yielding roughly 4-6%), supplemented by only a microscopic proportion of equities. Factoring this in, the maximum sensible return an insurer can offer clients hovers around 4.5%. This is precisely why actuaries act as the essential "gatekeepers" in product design—stripping away unrealistic fantasies to ensure absolute financial viability.

06 Bankruptcies: HIH & Equitable Life

To ensure everyone stays engaged, let's look at a simple actuarial thought experiment. Suppose I am underwriting 100 individuals. Statistics project that over the next year, exactly 1 person will suffer a broken leg, requiring a $10,000 payout. To break even on the risk alone, what is the absolute minimum premium I must collect from each of the 100 people? The arithmetic is straightforward: $10,000 divided by 100 people equals a Pure Premium of $100 per person. While this seems like elementary math, disaster strikes in the real world when corporate management overrides the actuary’s calculated bottom line.

A stark historical example is the collapse of the Australian giant, HIH Insurance. Despite actuaries knowing the true cost of risk, the company went bankrupt. Why? First, the $100 pure premium only covers the claim; the company must actually charge more to cover Operational Costs and agent commissions. Second, driven by a thirst for rapid expansion, the management engaged in a highly aggressive, predatory pricing war, deliberately selling policies below cost to seize Market Share, which left their reserves woefully inadequate. It is somewhat akin to modern tech startups burning through venture capital with massive consumer subsidies. However, an insurance company cannot operate on a "burn rate" model. Without continuous Capital Injection from a parent entity, an under-reserved insurer will inevitably collapse when a wave of claims hits.

Another classic cautionary tale is Equitable Life in the United States. During the 1990s, the US Macroeconomy was in a high-interest-rate environment, with yields easily hitting double digits. Equitable Life launched a flagship product offering a guaranteed 7% annual return—a seemingly conservative promise at the time. However, following massive sales, the US entered a prolonged Rate-cut Cycle, and market interest rates plummeted. Once it became impossible to find low-risk investment vehicles yielding over 7%, this flagship product became a massive financial hemorrhage. Unable to honor its guarantees to policyholders, the company's valuation crashed, and it was ultimately forced into an acquisition by MetLife.

Naturally, parents traditionally steer top students toward Medicine, Law, or trending fields like Engineering (AI) and Computing. So, where does the actuarial profession stand among the elite careers? According to the authoritative annual best jobs ranking by U.S. News & World Report, actuaries consistently rank in the top 11 overall professions, and an impressive 7th within the STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) category.

When evaluating a career, we must look far beyond the starting salary. We must holistically assess working hours, mental stress, and professional liability. For instance, construction workers tying rebar earn an exceptionally high daily wage, but the work is physically grueling under harsh weather. Doctors earn lucrative incomes and command high social status, but they endure extreme mental pressure, punishingly long hours, and face the constant threat of having their medical licenses revoked should a malpractice incident occur. Lawyers have immense earning potential, but often suffer through sleepless nights reviewing case files, and upon making partner, bear the heavy burden of constantly soliciting new clients.

In stark contrast, the operational framework for an actuary offers significantly more protection. In an insurance firm, critical actuarial numbers are never the sole responsibility of a single junior employee; your calculations undergo rigorous peer review, and the ultimate sign-off rests with the Chief Actuary. If you make a mathematical error, the worst-case scenario is losing your job—but your hard-earned professional license is not easily revoked. While an actuary's starting salary might not be the absolute highest across all industries (starting at a few hundred thousand HKD annually for a fresh graduate), once you accumulate four to five years of experience and attain your full Professional Qualification, an annual salary exceeding one million HKD (over $80,000 monthly) is highly common. If you desire a lucrative profession that does not require entertaining clients late into the night and offers an excellent Work-Life Balance, actuarial science is an outstanding choice.

08 Skills, Exams & Career Progression

The journey to becoming a fully qualified actuary is a marathon. Graduating with a four-year bachelor's degree is merely the starting line. Subsequently, you must balance a demanding full-time job with studying late into the night and on weekends to conquer notoriously difficult professional exams. If you give up halfway and fail to achieve your Fellowship (FSA) status, your career trajectory will likely hit a permanent ceiling at the Assistant Manager level. Therefore, you must possess profound dedication to mathematics and statistics.

Moreover, we must shatter the stereotype that an actuary is just a back-office number cruncher. In the early stages of your career, you will indeed handle massive data gathering and calculations. But as you ascend the corporate ladder, your core value shifts decisively toward cross-departmental communication and strategic business decision-making. When you present an Enterprise Risk Management report to the board, you must explain what Value at Risk (VaR) at a 99.5% Confidence Level implies for the company's capital. If you merely project a complex statistical distribution chart onto the screen, a CEO without an actuarial background will likely lose patience. You must hone exceptional communication skills, mastering the art of using accessible Layman Language to translate arcane mathematical models into actionable business metrics.

Regarding hard skills, Programming / Coding is now an indispensable competency for modern actuaries. Even if your code isn't flawless, you must be proficient in leveraging AI tools to assist in building programming frameworks. Concurrently, cultivating strong Business Sense—the ability to utilize data to strategically generate profit for the firm—is paramount.

In terms of qualification pathways, students in Hong Kong typically pursue credentials from either the Society of Actuaries (SOA) in the United States or the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries (IFoA) in the UK. I strongly advise laying a rock-solid foundation in pure mathematics and statistics during high school (such as taking the DSE M1/M2 extended modules). At the university level, the University of Hong Kong (HKU), the Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK), and City University of Hong Kong (CityU) offer premier actuarial science programs, while CUHK's Quantitative Finance (QFIN) is also a highly relevant alternative. Post-graduation, you will work and study concurrently to first attain your Associate (ASA) designation, before selecting a specialization (e.g., life insurance, investments) to achieve your Fellowship. This entire journey spans approximately 5 to 10 years. Fortunately, major insurance companies provide robust incentive systems: passing an exam typically earns you an automatic salary increment and generous Study Leave to support your academic progress.

09 Finance Challenge: Rates & Duration

As we approach the end of the lecture, let's synthesize what we've learned with a practical financial challenge. The financial health of an insurance company hinges entirely on its balance sheet. The company's Assets minus its Liabilities leaves the Equity, which belongs to the shareholders. Since the vast majority of an insurance company's capital is parked in bonds, we must understand how a bond's price is determined:

\[ \text{Bond Price} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{Cashflow}_t}{(1+r)^t} \]

Conceptually, this formula means we are taking the future Coupons and the principal to be received at maturity, and Discounting them back to their present value today. Here is the critical question: When the Market Interest Rate (r) decreases, does the Bond Price increase or decrease?

The answer is: The price increases. Looking mathematically at the formula, the interest rate 'r' sits in the denominator. When the interest rate falls (the denominator shrinks), the resulting bond price naturally inflates. Therefore, in a rate-cut environment, the value of the bond assets held by the insurance company surges. However, crucially, the present value of the company's future liabilities rises simultaneously.

So, does the company's overall Equity expand or contract? That entirely depends on whether the "assets grew more" or the "liabilities grew more." This introduces one of the most vital concepts in finance: Duration, which measures the sensitivity of an asset or liability to changes in interest rates. If the duration of the assets is greater than the duration of the liabilities, falling interest rates will cause the assets' value to outpace the liabilities' growth, resulting in an increase in Equity. Conversely, if the asset duration is lower, the Equity will shrink.

To definitively neutralize this systemic risk caused by interest rate volatility, an actuary's ultimate objective when crafting an investment strategy is strict Asset-Liability Matching (ALM). This means carefully calibrating the investment portfolio so that the duration of the assets perfectly matches the duration of the liabilities. By achieving this equilibrium, the company's net asset value remains rock-solid and impervious to turbulent shifts in the macroeconomic environment.

Furthermore, we are living in an era of technological explosion, and many students have grown accustomed to using Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools to complete their assignments. I am not opposed to leveraging AI to boost productivity—such as using it to debug or generate programming frameworks—but you must develop a clear "bottom-line" mentality. You must know exactly when it is detrimental to rely on AI. For instance, you absolutely cannot bring ChatGPT into the actual DSE examination hall.
