01 Opening and Speaker Introduction

他進一步向同學區分金融市場中的 sell-side 和 buy-side。Dominic 說明，sell-side 即「賣方」，例如投資銀行，主要向市場提供金融服務，包括交易執行、流動性提供、報價及其他金融服務；buy-side 即「買方」，則是真正管理資金和作出投資決策的一方，例如對沖基金、資產管理公司和其他投資機構。Dominic 後來轉到對沖基金工作，參與量化分析、策略設計、投資研究和風險管理相關工作。

Dominic 表示，當天講座主要有兩條主線。第一條主線是 risk management，即風險管理。他希望透過金融市場中的簡單例子，讓學生理解什麼是盈虧、如何拆解風險，以及統計模型在金融工作中的重要性。第二條主線是 artificial intelligence，即人工智能，尤其是大型語言模型、AI agent、tool calling、embedding、database、coding agent 等近年非常重要的概念。

02 Risk Management: Quantity, Price, PnL, and Market Examples

Dominic 隨後進入風險管理的主題。他指出，雖然 AI 的內容聽起來比較新奇，但由於課程本身與風險管理和精算有關，因此有必要先從金融市場中最基本的概念說起。

Dominic 解釋，在金融市場中，不論是在 buy-side 的對沖基金，還是在 sell-side 的投資銀行，從業員經常關心的核心問題都是：投資組合到底賺了多少、蝕了多少，以及為什麼賺錢或蝕錢。對風險管理而言，只看最後的總數是不足夠的，必須把盈虧來源拆解清楚。

\[\text{Value}=Q\times P\]

其中 \(Q\) 是 quantity，即持倉數量；\(P\) 是 price，即價格。Dominic 以股票作例子說明，如果投資者買入 100 股騰訊，股數乘以每股價格，就是該持倉的總市值。

Dominic 接着以 SK Hynix，即 SK 海力士，作為近期市場例子。他說明，SK Hynix 是韓國大型記憶體晶片製造商，近期因 AI data center、GPU 和 memory chip 的需求上升而備受市場關注。由於 AI data center 和高效能運算需要大量記憶體晶片，SK Hynix 因而成為市場焦點之一。

Dominic 亦提到，Samsung Electronics 和 SK Hynix 在韓國 KOSPI 指數中佔有相當重要的比重，因此投資這些公司某程度上亦代表投資者承擔了相當大的韓國股票市場 exposure。他進一步指出，有些市場參與者認為這些股票已經過熱，也有人認為其基本面，即 fundamentals，可以支持股價升幅，因為 AI infrastructure spending 帶來的需求是真實存在的。

在這個例子之後，Dominic 介紹 leverage，即槓桿。他解釋，如果投資者原本有 100 元，股票上升 10%，便賺取 10 元；但如果投資者借入額外 100 元，總共用 200 元買入股票，當股票上升 10% 時，便可賺取 20 元。這樣看起來回報被放大，但同時虧損亦會被放大。

Dominic 指出，金融市場中的槓桿不一定只來自直接借錢，亦可以透過衍生工具、槓桿 ETF、期權或其他金融產品形成。他提醒學生，槓桿在市場上升時可以放大收益，但在市場下跌時同樣會放大風險，因此它是風險管理中必須仔細處理的問題。

03 PnL Decomposition: Trading PnL and Market PnL

Dominic 接着由 \(Q\times P\) 公式進一步介紹 PnL，即 profit and loss，中文可理解為損益。他假設昨天的持倉是 \(Q_t\)，價格是 \(P_t\)；今天的持倉是 \(Q_{t+1}\)，價格是 \(P_{t+1}\)。因此，總 PnL 可以寫成：

\[\text{PnL}=Q_{t+1}P_{t+1}-Q_tP_t\]

Dominic 強調，雖然這條式子看似直接，但對風險管理來說仍然不夠清楚。因為持倉價值的變化可能來自兩件不同的事：第一，市場價格發生變化；第二，投資者自己增加或減少了持倉。因此，必須把這兩部分拆開。

他介紹 telescoping，即伸縮或套疊分解技巧。透過加減一項相同的項目，可以把 PnL 拆解為：

\[Q_{t+1}P_{t+1}-Q_tP_t=(Q_{t+1}P_{t+1}-Q_tP_{t+1})+(Q_tP_{t+1}-Q_tP_t)\]
\[\text{PnL}=(Q_{t+1}-Q_t)P_{t+1}+Q_t(P_{t+1}-P_t)\]

Dominic 解釋，第一部分 \((Q_{t+1}-Q_t)P_{t+1}\) 可以理解為 trading PnL，即因持倉數量改變而產生的部分；第二部分 \(Q_t(P_{t+1}-P_t)\) 則是 market PnL，即原有持倉因市場價格變動而產生的盈虧。

Dominic 用直觀例子說明，如果報表顯示投資組合價值上升，這未必代表投資策略真正賺了錢。它可能只是因為投資者買入了更多股票，令 portfolio size 變大。從公司整體角度來看，交易部門買入股票時，資金部 treasury 可能要撥出相應現金，因此交易部看到的持倉增加，不等於公司整體多賺了錢。

因此，Dominic 強調，PnL decomposition 是金融機構中非常基本但非常重要的工具。它使風險管理人員能夠把表面上的盈虧數字拆解成市場變動、交易行為和資金調配等不同來源，從而真正理解風險。

04 Market PnL, Model Price, and Model Risk

Dominic 隨後進一步討論 market PnL。他指出，market PnL 可以表示為：

\[\text{Market PnL}=Q_t\Delta P\]

其中 \(\Delta P=P_{t+1}-P_t\)，即市場價格變動。

Dominic 解釋，在金融建模中，風險管理人員不會只依賴市場觀察到的價格，而會建立一個模型價格：

\[\hat{P}_t=f(r_t,S_t,\sigma_t)\]

其中 \(f\) 是 pricing model，即定價模型；\(r_t\) 可以代表 interest rate，即利率；\(S_t\) 可以代表 stock price 或 underlying price，即相關資產價格；\(\sigma_t\) 則可以代表 volatility，即波動率。

Dominic 指出，市場給出的是 observed price，即實際價格 \(P_t\)，而模型給出的是 model price，即 \(\hat{P}_t\)。兩者未必完全相同。當價格變動時，可以把實際價格變動拆成模型解釋到的部分和模型解釋不到的部分：

\[\Delta P=\Delta\hat{P}+(\Delta P-\Delta\hat{P})\]

其中 \(\Delta\hat{P}\) 是 modelled move，即模型解釋到的價格變動；而 \(\Delta P-\Delta\hat{P}\) 則是 residual 或 model risk，即模型風險。

Dominic 強調，好的模型應該盡量令 model risk 接近零。如果 model risk 很大，代表市場價格變動和模型預測之間存在明顯差距。這時候就要檢查模型是否失效、是否有市場事件未被模型捕捉、是否有新聞、流動性變化、投資者行為或其他結構性因素影響價格。

他指出，在風險管理中，當投資組合蝕錢時，不能只說「市場跌了」。風險管理人員需要問：虧損是否來自利率變動？是否來自股票下跌？是否來自波動率上升？是否來自模型 calibration 不準？是否來自交易行為？還是來自 residual 或 model risk？這正是風險管理的核心精神。

05 Decomposing Modelled Market PnL into Risk Drivers

Dominic 接着示範如何把模型價格變動進一步拆解成不同 risk drivers 的貢獻。假設模型價格是：

\[\hat{P}_t=f(r_t,S_t,\sigma_t)\]

由 \(t\) 到 \(t+1\) 的模型價格變動可以寫成：

\[\Delta\hat{P}=f(r_{t+1},S_{t+1},\sigma_{t+1}) - f(r_t,S_t,\sigma_t)\]

Dominic 說明，可以再次使用 telescoping 的想法，逐步改變模型中的 input。首先只改變利率，再改變股價，最後改變波動率：

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta\hat{P} =&\;\left[f(r_{t+1},S_t,\sigma_t)-f(r_t,S_t,\sigma_t)\right]\\ &+\left[f(r_{t+1},S_{t+1},\sigma_t)-f(r_{t+1},S_t,\sigma_t)\right]\\ &+\left[f(r_{t+1},S_{t+1},\sigma_{t+1})-f(r_{t+1},S_{t+1},\sigma_t)\right] \end{aligned} \]

第一項可以理解為 rate effect，即利率變動的影響；第二項是 stock effect，即股價變動的影響；第三項是 volatility effect，即波動率變動的影響。

Dominic 強調，這種分解方法的價值在於讓投資者知道 PnL 來自哪裏。如果今天投資組合蝕錢，而分解結果顯示主要來自利率變動，那就要考慮是否需要利率對沖；如果主要來自波動率變化，就要檢查 volatility exposure；如果主要來自 residual，就可能代表模型本身需要重新檢查。

Dominic 提醒同學，風險管理的重點不是永遠不輸錢，而是知道自己在什麼風險上輸錢，知道這些風險是否原本預期會承擔，以及知道是否需要 hedge。

06 Why Models Fail and Why AI Becomes Relevant

Dominic 進一步指出，過去二十多年，傳統金融模型在正常市場環境下大多能有效運作。當市場因素相對穩定時，價格變化主要由利率、股價、波動率等變數驅動，模型便能夠捕捉主要風險。

不過，Dominic 指出，現代市場比以往更複雜。價格可能受到新聞、政策、投資者情緒、社交媒體、總統選舉、地緣政治、季節性、供應鏈消息等大量因素影響。很多資訊並不是結構化數字，而是文字、新聞、報告和市場敘事。

以 AI 晶片股為例，Dominic 指出，它們的升幅不只是由過去價格數據解釋，而是與市場對未來 AI data center spending 的預期、公司財報、供應鏈、技術突破、宏觀利率環境等因素有關。這些因素往往藏在大量文字資料之中。

因此，Dominic 認為，現代金融分析越來越需要能夠讀取大量文字資訊的模型，例如新聞、研究報告、公告、財報、分析師評論和社交媒體資料。大型語言模型能夠處理文字資料、摘要資訊、找出主題和建立語義關聯，因此在風險管理和金融分析中具有潛力。

07 Why Statistics Still Matters in the Age of AI

Dominic 接着回應一個重要問題：既然 AI 已經如此強大，統計學是否仍然重要？他的觀點是，統計學不但仍然重要，而且在 AI 時代可能更加重要。

Dominic 用傳統統計學的 workflow 說明：

\[\text{Data}\rightarrow\text{Statistical Model}\rightarrow\text{Metrics}\rightarrow\text{Data}\]

統計工作通常先有數據，然後建立模型，再用 metrics 評估模型表現，例如 forecast error、accuracy、RMSE、backtesting PnL 等。評估之後，再根據結果修正模型或收集更多數據，形成一個循環。

Dominic 指出，AI system 其實也有類似循環：

\[\text{Data}\rightarrow\text{Agent}\rightarrow\text{Feedback}\rightarrow\text{Data}\]

當資料輸入 agent 後，agent 透過大型語言模型和 tools 產生回應或執行任務，之後產生 feedback，而這些 feedback 又可以成為下一輪改進的資料。

因此，Dominic 強調，真正厲害的 AI 系統不只是單次 prompt 和 response，而是能建立一個有效 loop。它可以不斷讀取資料、執行任務、接收評估、修正輸出，甚至改善工作流程。

Dominic 以金融中的 backtesting 作類比。投資策略不能只因一次成功就被認為可靠，而要看它在歷史資料和不同情境中是否穩定。AI 亦一樣，不能只看一次回答是否漂亮，而要透過 evaluation loop 檢查其穩定性、可靠性、錯誤率和是否會 hallucinate。

08 Large Language Models, Distillation, and the Current AI Landscape

Dominic 隨後介紹大型語言模型的現況。他提到同學可能聽過 ChatGPT、Claude、Gemini、Kimi、DeepSeek、Zhipu GLM 等模型。不同模型各有特點，有些 reasoning 能力較強，有些 coding 較好，有些價格較低，有些 latency 較小，也有些在中文或香港使用環境中比較方便。

Dominic 解釋，大型語言模型可以簡化理解為：模型根據大量文本訓練，學習在某個上下文中下一個 token 最可能是什麼。它逐步生成文字，每一個 token 都根據上下文和模型參數計算而來。

他亦說明，模型可以大致分成 foundation model 和 distilled model。Foundation model 是由大量資料和 compute 從頭訓練而成的基礎模型；distilled model 則是透過知識蒸餾，把大型模型產生的答案用來訓練較小、成本較低的模型。

Dominic 提醒同學，benchmark 分數高不代表實際使用時一定最好。很多模型在標準題目上表現相近，但在長文組織、多步推理、工具調用、資料庫查詢、代碼修復和企業知識管理等複雜任務中，差異就會顯現。

因此，Dominic 認為，與其只問「哪個模型最強」，更重要的是看整個 AI system 是否設計得好，包括 tools 是否清楚、context 是否足夠、evaluation 是否可靠、cost 是否可控，以及 security 是否合格。

09 Chatbot versus AI Agent

Dominic 接着說明 chatbot 和 AI agent 的分別。他指出，很多人會把兩者混為一談，但兩者其實有重要差異。

普通 chatbot 主要根據使用者輸入生成文字回答，可以解釋概念、改寫文章、回答問題或整理資料。但如果它沒有外部工具，通常不能真正執行任務，例如不能查公司資料庫、不能搜尋最新網頁、不能發電郵、不能生成圖表，也不能執行程式。

Dominic 用一條簡單公式概括 AI agent：

\[\text{Agent}=\text{LLM}+\text{Tools}\]

LLM 是大腦，而 tools 是它的手腳和感官。Dominic 指出，如果給 agent web search tool，它可以查最新資料；如果給它 database query tool，它可以查 SQL database；如果給它 code execution tool，它可以寫程式和讀錯誤訊息；如果給它 charting tool，它可以生成圖表；如果給它 memory tool，它可以找回過去對話或文件。

Dominic 強調，一個 agent 的能力不只取決於模型本身，而是取決於 tools 的設計。Tools 太少，它做不到事；tools 太多而沒有清楚指引，它會混亂。因此，好的 agent system 需要恰當工具、清楚 context、合理 system prompt、可靠 guardrails，以及可檢查 evaluation。

10 Embeddings, Vector Data, Qdrant, and AI Memory

Dominic 進一步介紹 AI 時代另一類重要資料：vector data。他首先解釋 embedding，即把文字轉換成一串數字向量。講義中使用 SentenceTransformer 的 all-MiniLM-L6-v2 作例子，可把文字轉成 384 維向量。

Dominic 說明，這些數字不是隨機產生，而是代表文字語義的位置。意思相近的文字，轉成 vector 後在高維空間中會比較接近。例如 “risk management” 和 “managing risk” 語義相似，因此它們的 vector 會較接近。

為了量度兩個 vector 是否相似，Dominic 介紹 cosine similarity：

\[\cos(v,w)=\frac{v\cdot w}{\lVert v\rVert\lVert w\rVert}\]

當 cosine similarity 接近 1，代表兩者語義相似度高。Dominic 指出，這比傳統 keyword search 更強，因為 embedding search 可以找語義相近的內容，而不只找完全相同的字。

Dominic 解釋，如果公司有大量新聞、報告、研究筆記、交易記錄或會議摘要，可以先把文字切成 chunks，再轉成 embeddings，存入 vector database，例如 Qdrant。當使用者提出新問題時，系統可以把問題也轉成 vector，再找出最相似的內容，放入 agent 的 context。

講義中的 OpenClaw memory: vector path 也可以用這種方式理解：memory notes 先經過 chunk 和 embed，放入 vector index；新問題再經過 query embedding，找出 nearest chunks，最後放入 agent context。

11 Database, SQL, DuckDB, Postgres, and S3

Dominic 接着從文字資料轉到 structured data。他指出，很多學生平日常用 Excel，但在企業和金融機構，資料通常不會只放在 Excel，而會放在 database，例如 PostgreSQL、DuckDB 或其他 SQL database。

Dominic 解釋，SQL 是 Structured Query Language，用來查詢和操作資料庫。例如可以用： 

SELECT * FROM trades LIMIT 10;

查看 trades table 的前十行。講義亦提到，DuckDB 可以查詢本地資料、Postgres connection string，以及 S3 上的 parquet files。

Dominic 指出，AI agent 如果能夠連接 database，就不只是回答文字問題，而是能真正查資料。例如使用者可以問「今年每月銷售趨勢如何」、「哪個產品收入最高」、「請比較不同地區銷售表現」或「請畫一張 revenue line chart」。如果 agent 知道 database schema，又有 SQL query tool，就可以自動生成 SQL、查詢資料、整理結果，甚至調用 charting tool 畫圖。

12 Assistant UI Demo: Building a Chatbot Interface

Dominic 之後示範如何建立一個基本 chatbot interface。他使用 Assistant UI 作為例子。Assistant UI 是一個開源前端工具，可以快速 scaffold 一個 React / Next.js chat app。講義中列出相關指令，例如： 

npx assistant-ui@latest create my-app
cd my-app

亦可選擇 minimal template 或 with-ai-sdk-v7 example。

Dominic 解釋，這個示範的目的，是讓學生看到現代 AI app 的 user interface 很多時已經有 open source template 可以使用。以前要做一個類似 ChatGPT、有 sidebar、conversation history、message bubbles 和 streaming response 的介面，可能需要花很多時間；現在透過開源框架，可以很快建立 prototype。

Dominic 亦介紹 GitHub。他指出，GitHub 是開發者常用的平台，很多 open source project 都可以在上面找到。學生可以學習如何閱讀 documentation、查看 stars、觀察 community activity、理解 issues，以及判斷一個 library 是否成熟。

13 Connecting the Chatbot to an LLM Provider

在建立 Assistant UI 的介面後，Dominic 示範如何接駁 LLM provider。他指出，很多模板預設使用 OpenAI API，但在不同地區或使用環境中，可能會選擇其他 provider。示範中，他嘗試把原本使用 OpenAI 的部分改成 Kimi API。

Dominic 解釋，這裏涉及 API key 和 endpoint。API key 可以理解為使用服務的鑰匙，而 endpoint 則是模型服務的接口。App 會把使用者訊息、system prompt、tools definition 等傳送到 LLM provider，再由模型回傳回答。

在 live demo 中，Dominic 讓 coding agent 協助修改 codebase，把原本使用 OpenAI 的部分改成 Kimi。這展示了現代 coding workflow 的轉變：以前遇到問題可能要查 documentation、Google 或 Stack Overflow；現在則可以讓 coding agent 讀取 codebase、修改檔案、安裝 package 和處理 error。

不過，Dominic 亦提醒學生，這不是 magic。API key 可能設定錯，environment variable 可能缺失，endpoint 可能不正確，package 可能未安裝。因此，即使有 AI 幫助，開發者仍然需要理解基本 debugging 思路。

14 Adding Web Search Tool: From LLM to Agent

Dominic 接着示範如何把 chatbot 變成更接近 agent 的系統。一開始建立的 chatbot 只是 LLM，可以回答一些常識性問題；但如果問它「SK Hynix 最新股價是多少」，模型本身未必知道，因為它沒有即時市場資料。

因此，Dominic 示範加入 web search tool。當 agent 需要最新資訊時，就可以 trigger tool call，搜尋網頁，再根據搜尋結果回答。

Dominic 強調，如果 AI 答不到，不一定是模型不聰明，可能只是因為它沒有工具。就像人類如果不能上網、不能查資料庫、不能用 calculator，即使思考能力很強，也未必能回答最新價格或做精確計算。

在示範中，加入 web search tool 後，agent 開始懂得進行 tool call。不過，tool call 也會出錯，例如搜尋結果為空、API 回傳格式不符合要求，或工具本身設定不完整。Dominic 指出，這些都是真實開發 AI agent 時會遇到的問題。

15 Coding Agent and Pair Coding

Dominic 亦示範了 coding agent 的使用。他指出，coding agent 和普通 chatbot 的最大分別，是 coding agent 可以使用開發工具，例如讀取 codebase、搜尋檔案、修改程式碼、執行 terminal command、安裝 npm package、讀取 compiler error，並根據錯誤再修正。

Dominic 把這種工作方式比喻為 pair coding。傳統 pair programming 是兩個人一起寫程式，一個人寫，一個人看，互相檢查。Coding agent 某程度上可以成為開發者的 pair programmer，協助嘗試、修改、檢查和 debug。

他同時提醒學生，coding fundamentals 仍然重要。開發者仍然需要理解 project structure、frontend 和 backend 的溝通方式、API route、package.json、TypeScript error、localhost、environment variable，以及 database connection 為什麼會 fail。AI 可以加快速度，但理解系統仍然是人類的責任。

16 Building a Company Sales Database with Postgres and Docker

Dominic 之後示範一個更接近企業應用的例子：建立一個公司銷售資料庫，讓 agent 可以查詢。示範中假設公司有幾個 tables，例如 products、sales 和 sale_items。

Dominic 說明，這些資料可以放在 PostgreSQL database 中。為了在本機快速建立資料庫環境，他使用 Docker。Docker 可以把 database 和運行環境放在 container 中，方便在不同電腦上重現。

Dominic 解釋，Docker image 包含了執行某個服務需要的環境；當開發者 run 這個 image，就會產生一個 container。這個 container 就像一個獨立的小環境，可以運行 Postgres、web server 或其他服務。

示範中，Dominic 讓 Postgres 在本機某個 port 上運行，例如 5433，然後 app 或 agent 可以透過 connection string 連接 database。Dominic 強調，真正有用的 AI agent 通常要連接真實資料來源，例如公司 database、internal documents、workflow systems 或 financial models，而不只是回答百科式問題。

17 Giving the Agent Database Schema as Context

Dominic 接着指出，如果 agent 要查 database，它不只是需要 SQL tool，還需要知道 schema。Schema 是資料庫的結構，例如有哪些 tables、每個 table 有哪些 columns，以及 tables 之間如何關聯。

例如 products table 可能有 product_id、name、sku、category；sales table 可能有 sale_id、sold_at、customer_id；sale_items table 則可能有 sale_id、product_id、quantity、unit_price。

Dominic 解釋，如果 agent 不知道 schema，即使有 database tool，也可能查錯 table 或 column。因此，在示範中，他把 schema 加入 system prompt 或 agent context，讓 agent 每次回答問題時都知道可用的資料結構。

Dominic 指出，這就是 context engineering。相比單純寫一句 prompt，AI agent 更需要完整、結構化和準確的 context，包括背景資料、工具說明、資料庫結構、使用限制和回答規則。

18 Querying Sales Data and Generating Charts

當 agent 有 database tool 和 schema context 後，Dominic 示範它如何回答銷售資料相關問題。例如使用者可以要求 agent 總結 sales data、找出銷售額最高的產品、計算總收入，或畫出每月銷售趨勢。

Dominic 解釋，agent 可以先生成 SQL，查詢 Postgres，再把結果整理成人類可讀的文字。如果進一步加入 charting tool，例如前端圖表 library，agent 就可以把結果視覺化，生成 line chart 或 bar chart。

Dominic 用這個示範說明一個完整 AI workflow：使用者提出自然語言問題；agent 判斷需要查 database；agent 根據 schema 生成 SQL；SQL tool 查詢 Postgres；agent 接收結果；agent 用文字解釋；如有需要，再調用 charting tool 顯示圖表。

這展示了 AI 在企業環境中的實際價值。Dominic 強調，AI 不只是「聊天」，而是能把自然語言、資料庫、分析工具和視覺化整合成一個可執行的 workflow。

19 System Prompt, Tool Calling, Guardrails, and Evaluation

Dominic 透過整個示範說明 system prompt 的重要性。當使用者在畫面輸入一句話時，真正送到 LLM 的不只是該句話，系統背後通常還會附加 agent 的角色、可用 tools、tool 輸入格式、database schema、回答規則、失敗處理方式和安全限制。

Dominic 指出，這些設計是 AI system 的核心。沒有清楚 system prompt 和 tool instructions，agent 即使有工具也可能用錯。

Dominic 亦介紹 guardrails 的概念。Guardrails 可以理解為安全欄杆或控制機制，用來避免 AI 做出不合理行為，例如 tool call 回傳空結果時不應亂作答案、資料庫沒有某個 table 時不應假裝查到、API key 不存在時應該指出設定問題、不確定時應該說明限制。

另一個重要概念是 evaluation。Dominic 指出，AI system 必須可測試，例如 SQL 是否正確、圖表是否反映資料、答案是否有根據、tool call 是否合理、成本是否過高、latency 是否可接受。

20 GitHub Copilot and Coding IDEs

Dominic 其後提到 GitHub Copilot 和 coding IDE。他指出，如 Visual Studio Code 這類編輯器現在已可接入 coding agent，而 GitHub Copilot 是常見例子之一。

這些 coding agent 可以幫助開發者補全程式碼、生成 function、解釋 error、重構程式、寫 test、根據 project context 修改檔案、接駁 API 和修改 frontend component。

Dominic 強調，使用者仍然要懂得指揮 AI，也要能判斷 AI 生成的 code 是否合理。AI 可能做錯、使用過時 library、忽略安全問題或漏掉 edge cases。因此，未來學 coding 不只是學 syntax，而是要理解整個 software system。

21 Mac, Windows, Linux, and WSL

Dominic 亦與同學討論不同電腦環境。他指出，很多 AI、database、backend 和 distributed computing 工具，在 Linux 或類 Unix 環境上支援最好。Mac 和 Linux 在 terminal、file system、command line 和 development workflow 上比較接近。

Dominic 說明，Windows 當然也可以做開發，但某些工具在 Windows 上會較麻煩，特別是涉及 multiprocessing、forking、asynchronous programming、server-side libraries 或 Linux-native tools 時。

因此，Dominic 建議使用 Windows 的學生可以學習 WSL，即 Windows Subsystem for Linux。WSL 可讓使用者在 Windows 中運行 Linux 環境，對學習 modern development workflow 很有幫助。

22 AI in Education: Easier or Harder?

在問答環節中，有同學問 AI 會令學習變容易還是變困難。Dominic 回應，AI 會令表面上的完成任務變容易，但也可能令真正的學習差距變大。

Dominic 指出，如果學生只是把功課交給 AI 完成，短期內可能很快交出漂亮答案，但如果沒有真正理解，到了考試、面試、專題答辯或實際工作時，就會發現自己其實不懂。

Dominic 提到，在大學課程中亦出現類似情況。有些功課可以使用 AI，或者很難完全禁止使用 AI，因此很多同學功課分數很高，但到 closed-book examination 時，才發現自己沒有真正掌握。

相反，Dominic 認為，如果學生把 AI 當作學習夥伴，用它來問問題、追問原因、比較方法、生成練習、檢查錯誤和解釋概念，AI 就可以令學習更快、更深。

23 Coding Fundamentals in the AI Era

Dominic 亦回應了「既然 AI 可以寫程式，是否仍然需要學 coding」這個問題。他的看法是，coding fundamentals 仍然重要，但學習重點會改變。

Dominic 指出，以前學 coding 可能很注重逐行 syntax；現在 AI 可以生成很多 boilerplate code，也可以協助修改錯誤。不過，學生仍然需要理解程式如何運行、server 如何啟動、frontend 如何連接 backend、API endpoint 是什麼、database 如何查詢、authentication 如何處理、package dependency 如何管理、deployment 如何進行，以及 error message 如何閱讀。

Dominic 認為，未來 coding 更像 system thinking。開發者不只是寫一個 function，而是設計一個 workflow。AI 可以幫忙完成很多細節，但人仍然要知道整體方向和檢查標準。

24 AI Bubble, Chips, Leverage, and Market Risk

在最後的問答中，有同學問 AI bubble 是否會爆破。Dominic 認為，這是一個很好的問題，應把 AI market 拆成幾個部分來看。

第一部分是 chips，即晶片。Dominic 指出，AI 需要大量計算資源，因此市場高度關注 NVIDIA、TSMC、SK Hynix、Samsung Electronics 等與 GPU、memory 和 semiconductor supply chain 相關的公司。只要 AI data center spending 持續，晶片和 memory 的需求就有一定基本面支持。

不過，Dominic 亦提醒同學，市場可能有過熱和槓桿問題。當很多投資者使用 leverage，一旦股票下跌，就可能出現 forced selling 或 margin call，令市場波動加劇。

他用槓桿作例子說明，如果投資者使用三倍槓桿，標的資產下跌約三分之一時，投資者的本金可能幾乎全數損失；若槓桿更高，較小跌幅亦可能造成嚴重損失。

25 SaaS, Enterprise Software, and AI Disruption

Dominic 接着討論 SaaS，即 software as a service。他指出，過去很多公司靠軟件訂閱收費，例如 CRM、analytics platform、presentation tools、design software 和 office tools。

AI 出現後，有些人認為部分 SaaS 會受到挑戰。Dominic 舉例，如果 AI agent 可以直接生成 slides、分析 sales data、寫報告、整理 email 和處理 workflow，使用者是否仍然需要那麼多獨立 software，便成為值得思考的問題。

不過，Dominic 亦指出，這不代表所有 SaaS 都會消失。企業仍然需要權限管理、compliance、security、audit trail、data integration、workflow management、deployment support、user management、reliability 和 enterprise governance。

Dominic 認為，未來的競爭不是單純「有沒有 AI」，而是誰能把 AI 做成可靠、安全、可控、可部署的 enterprise system。

26 The Real Future: LLM + Tools + Context + Workflow

Dominic 在總結中指出，未來 AI 的核心不是單一模型，而是：

\[\text{LLM}+\text{Tools}+\text{Context}+\text{Workflow}+\text{Evaluation}\]

如果只有 LLM，它只是一個會說話的模型；如果加上 tools，它可以做事；如果加上 context，它知道在什麼環境下做事；如果加上 workflow，它可以持續完成任務；如果加上 evaluation，它才有機會可靠。

Dominic 認為，對學生而言，最重要的不是追逐每一個最新工具，而是建立基本能力，包括數學、統計、金融常識、編程思維、AI literacy、data literacy、problem formulation、critical thinking 和 communication。

02 Risk Management: Quantity, Price, PnL, and Market Examples

Dominic then moved into the topic of risk management. He pointed out that although AI might sound more exciting and fashionable, the programme itself was closely related to risk management and actuarial science. Therefore, it was important to begin with some fundamental ideas from financial markets.

He explained that in financial markets, whether one works on the buy-side at a hedge fund or on the sell-side at an investment bank, a central question is always: How much money did the portfolio make or lose, and why? From a risk management perspective, it is not enough to look only at the final number. One must understand where the profit or loss came from.

\[\text{Value}=Q\times P\]

Here, \(Q\) stands for quantity, meaning the number of units or shares held, while \(P\) stands for price. For example, if an investor holds 100 shares of Tencent, then the number of shares multiplied by the share price gives the total market value of that holding.

Dominic then used SK Hynix as a current market example. SK Hynix is a major South Korean memory chip manufacturer. He explained that the company had recently attracted significant market attention because of the increasing demand for AI data centers, GPUs, and memory chips. Since AI data centers and high-performance computing systems require large amounts of memory chips, SK Hynix has become one of the companies closely watched by investors.

He also mentioned that SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics account for an important portion of South Korea’s KOSPI index. Therefore, investing in these companies may also mean taking on a large amount of exposure to the South Korean equity market. Dominic noted that some market participants believe these stocks have become overheated, while others argue that their fundamentals may justify the price increases because the demand created by AI infrastructure spending is real.

After this example, Dominic introduced the concept of leverage. He explained that if an investor originally has 100 dollars and a stock rises by 10%, the investor earns 10 dollars. However, if the investor borrows another 100 dollars and invests a total of 200 dollars, then a 10% increase in the stock price produces a 20-dollar gain. In this case, the return on the original 100 dollars of capital appears to be magnified.

Dominic emphasized that leverage in financial markets does not only come from directly borrowing money. It can also be created through derivatives, leveraged ETFs, options, or other financial products. He reminded students that leverage can magnify gains when markets rise, but it can also magnify losses when markets fall. For this reason, leverage is one of the key issues that risk management must handle carefully.

03 PnL Decomposition: Trading PnL and Market PnL

Dominic then moved from the formula \(Q\times P\) to the concept of PnL, which stands for profit and loss. He considered a simple situation: suppose yesterday’s position was \(Q_t\) and yesterday’s price was \(P_t\), while today’s position is \(Q_{t+1}\) and today’s price is \(P_{t+1}\). The total PnL can be written as:

\[\text{PnL}=Q_{t+1}P_{t+1}-Q_tP_t\]

This represents today’s position value minus yesterday’s position value.

Dominic stressed that while this formula looks straightforward, it is still not detailed enough for risk management. The change in value may come from two different sources. First, the market price may have changed. Second, the investor may have increased or reduced the position size. These two effects should be separated.

He introduced a mathematical technique called telescoping decomposition. By adding and subtracting the same term, the PnL can be decomposed as follows:

\[Q_{t+1}P_{t+1}-Q_tP_t=(Q_{t+1}P_{t+1}-Q_tP_{t+1})+(Q_tP_{t+1}-Q_tP_t)\]
\[\text{PnL}=(Q_{t+1}-Q_t)P_{t+1}+Q_t(P_{t+1}-P_t)\]

Dominic explained that the first part, \((Q_{t+1}-Q_t)P_{t+1}\), can be understood as trading PnL. It reflects the part of the change caused by changes in the quantity held. The second part, \(Q_t(P_{t+1}-P_t)\), is market PnL. It reflects the profit or loss caused by market price movements on the original position.

Dominic used an intuitive example to explain the importance of this decomposition. If a report shows that the value of a portfolio has increased, this does not necessarily mean that the investment strategy has made money. It may simply mean that the investor bought more shares and increased the size of the portfolio. From the perspective of the whole company, when the trading desk buys more shares, the treasury department may have to provide the corresponding cash. Therefore, an increase in the trading book does not automatically mean that the whole company has made more money.

Dominic emphasized that PnL decomposition is a basic but extremely important tool in financial institutions. It allows risk managers to separate the overall profit and loss figure into different sources, such as market movement, trading activity, and funding effects. Only then can one properly understand the risks being taken.

04 Market PnL, Model Price, and Model Risk

Dominic then discussed market PnL in greater detail. Market PnL can be expressed as:

\[\text{Market PnL}=Q_t\Delta P\]

where \(\Delta P=P_{t+1}-P_t\), representing the change in market price.

Dominic explained that in financial modelling, risk managers do not rely only on observed market prices. They often build a model price:

\[\hat{P}_t=f(r_t,S_t,\sigma_t)\]

Here, \(f\) represents a pricing model. The variable \(r_t\) may represent the interest rate, \(S_t\) may represent the stock price or underlying price, and \(\sigma_t\) may represent volatility.

Dominic pointed out that the market gives the observed price \(P_t\), while the model gives the model price \(\hat{P}_t\). These two prices may not be exactly the same. When the price changes, the actual price change can be separated into the part that the model explains and the part that the model does not explain:

\[\Delta P=\Delta\hat{P}+(\Delta P-\Delta\hat{P})\]

Here, \(\Delta\hat{P}\) is the modelled move, meaning the price change explained by the model. The remaining part, \(\Delta P-\Delta\hat{P}\), is the residual or model risk.

Dominic emphasized that a good model should keep model risk as close to zero as possible. If model risk is large, it means that the actual market price movement is very different from what the model predicts. In that case, one must examine whether the model has failed, whether a market event has not been captured, or whether news, liquidity changes, investor behavior, or other structural factors have affected the price.

He explained that in risk management, when a portfolio loses money, it is not enough to say, “The market went down.” Risk managers need to ask whether the loss came from interest rate changes, stock price movements, volatility increases, poor model calibration, trading activity, or residual model risk. This is the core spirit of risk management.

05 Decomposing Modelled Market PnL into Risk Drivers

Dominic then showed how the modelled price change can be further decomposed into different risk drivers. Suppose the model price is:

\[\hat{P}_t=f(r_t,S_t,\sigma_t)\]

The model price change from time \(t\) to time \(t+1\) can be written as:

\[\Delta\hat{P}=f(r_{t+1},S_{t+1},\sigma_{t+1}) - f(r_t,S_t,\sigma_t)\]

Dominic explained that the same telescoping idea can be used again. The inputs can be changed step by step: first the interest rate, then the stock price, and finally volatility.

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta\hat{P} =&\;\left[f(r_{t+1},S_t,\sigma_t)-f(r_t,S_t,\sigma_t)\right]\\ &+\left[f(r_{t+1},S_{t+1},\sigma_t)-f(r_{t+1},S_t,\sigma_t)\right]\\ &+\left[f(r_{t+1},S_{t+1},\sigma_{t+1})-f(r_{t+1},S_{t+1},\sigma_t)\right] \end{aligned} \]

The first term can be interpreted as the rate effect, the impact of changes in interest rates. The second term is the stock effect, the impact of changes in the stock or underlying price. The third term is the volatility effect, the impact of changes in volatility.

Dominic stressed that the value of this decomposition is that it helps investors understand where PnL comes from. If a portfolio loses money and the decomposition shows that most of the loss came from interest rate movement, then the team may consider interest rate hedging. If the loss came mainly from volatility changes, then the volatility exposure should be examined. If the loss came mainly from residual risk, then the model itself may need to be reviewed.

Dominic reminded students that risk management is not about never losing money. It is about knowing which risks one is losing money on, whether those risks were intended, and whether hedging is needed.

06 Why Models Fail and Why AI Becomes Relevant

Dominic then explained that over the past twenty years or so, many traditional financial models have worked reasonably well under normal market conditions. When market factors are relatively stable, price changes are often driven mainly by variables such as interest rates, stock prices, and volatility. In those situations, models can capture the major risks.

However, Dominic pointed out that modern markets are more complex than before. Prices may be affected by news, policies, investor sentiment, social media, presidential elections, geopolitics, seasonality, and supply chain developments. Much of this information is not structured numerical data. It appears instead in text, news reports, research notes, company announcements, and market narratives.

Using AI chip stocks as an example, Dominic explained that their price movements cannot be understood only through past price data. They are also linked to the market’s expectations about future AI data center spending, company earnings, supply chains, technological breakthroughs, and the macro interest rate environment. These factors are often embedded in large amounts of text data.

Therefore, Dominic argued that modern financial analysis increasingly requires models that can process large volumes of textual information, such as news articles, research reports, announcements, earnings reports, analyst comments, and social media data. Large language models are powerful in processing text, summarizing information, identifying themes, and building semantic connections. For this reason, they may be useful in risk management and financial analysis.

07 Why Statistics Still Matters in the Age of AI

Dominic then addressed an important question: If AI has become so powerful, does statistics still matter? His view was that statistics not only remains important, but may become even more important in the AI era.

He described the traditional statistical workflow as:

\[\text{Data}\rightarrow\text{Statistical Model}\rightarrow\text{Metrics}\rightarrow\text{Data}\]

In statistical work, one begins with data, builds a model, evaluates the model using metrics such as forecast error, accuracy, RMSE, or backtesting PnL, and then uses the results to improve the model or collect more data. This forms a loop.

Dominic explained that AI systems follow a similar loop:

\[\text{Data}\rightarrow\text{Agent}\rightarrow\text{Feedback}\rightarrow\text{Data}\]

Data is given to an agent. The agent uses a large language model and tools to produce responses or perform tasks. The feedback then becomes data for the next round of improvement.

Dominic emphasized that a truly powerful AI system is not just a single prompt and a single response. It is a loop that can continuously read information, perform tasks, receive evaluation, improve its outputs, and sometimes refine its workflow.

He compared this with backtesting in finance. An investment strategy should not be considered reliable simply because it worked once. It has to be tested across historical data and different scenarios. AI systems should be evaluated in a similar way. One should not judge an AI system only by whether one answer looks impressive. One must test whether it is stable, reliable, error-aware, and resistant to hallucination.

08 Large Language Models, Distillation, and the Current AI Landscape

Dominic then introduced the current landscape of large language models. He mentioned that students might have heard of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Kimi, DeepSeek, Zhipu GLM, and other models. Different models have different strengths. Some are stronger in reasoning, some are better at coding, some are cheaper, some have lower latency, and some are more convenient in Chinese-language or Hong Kong usage environments.

Dominic explained that a large language model can be loosely understood as a model trained on large amounts of text to predict the most likely next token in a given context. It generates text step by step, with each token determined by the previous context and the model parameters.

He also explained two broad categories of models: foundation models and distilled models. Foundation models are trained from the ground up using large amounts of data and compute. Distilled models, on the other hand, are trained by using the answers produced by larger models to train a smaller and cheaper model.

Dominic reminded students that a high benchmark score does not necessarily mean that a model will perform best in real-world use. Many models may perform similarly on standard tests, but differences become clear when they are used for complex tasks such as long-form organization, multi-step reasoning, tool calling, database querying, code repair, and enterprise knowledge management.

Therefore, Dominic argued that instead of only asking “Which model is the strongest?”, it is more important to ask whether the whole AI system is well designed. Are the tools clear? Is the context sufficient? Is the evaluation reliable? Is the cost controllable? Is the system secure?

09 Chatbot versus AI Agent

Dominic then explained the distinction between a chatbot and an AI agent. He pointed out that many people use the two terms interchangeably, but they are not the same.

A normal chatbot mainly generates text responses based on user input. It can explain concepts, rewrite text, answer questions, or organize information. However, without external tools, it usually cannot truly perform actions. It cannot query a company database, search the latest web information, send emails, generate charts, or execute code.

Dominic summarized the idea of an AI agent with a simple formula:

\[\text{Agent}=\text{LLM}+\text{Tools}\]

The LLM is the brain, while tools act as the hands, legs, and senses. If an agent is given a web search tool, it can look up updated information. If it is given a database query tool, it can query an SQL database. If it is given a code execution tool, it can write code and read error messages. If it is given a charting tool, it can generate charts. If it is given a memory tool, it can retrieve previous conversations or documents.

Dominic emphasized that the power of an agent depends not only on the model itself, but also on the design of its tools. If there are too few tools, it cannot do much. If there are too many tools without clear instructions, it may become confused. A good agent system needs suitable tools, clear context, a well-designed system prompt, reliable guardrails, and evaluation mechanisms.

10 Embeddings, Vector Data, Qdrant, and AI Memory

Dominic then introduced another important type of data in the AI era: vector data. He first explained embeddings, which convert text into numerical vectors. The handout used SentenceTransformer’s all-MiniLM-L6-v2 as an example, which can convert text into a 384-dimensional vector.

Dominic explained that these numbers are not random. They represent the semantic position of the text. Texts with similar meanings will be close to each other in the high-dimensional vector space. For example, “risk management” and “managing risk” have similar meanings, so their vectors should be close.

To measure the similarity between two vectors, Dominic introduced cosine similarity:

\[\cos(v,w)=\frac{v\cdot w}{\lVert v\rVert\lVert w\rVert}\]

When cosine similarity is close to 1, the two pieces of text are semantically similar. Dominic pointed out that this is more powerful than traditional keyword search because embedding search can find content with similar meanings, not only content that uses exactly the same words.

Dominic explained that if a company has many news articles, reports, research notes, trading records, or meeting summaries, these texts can first be divided into chunks, converted into embeddings, and stored in a vector database such as Qdrant. When a user asks a new question, the system converts the question into a vector and retrieves the most similar content to place into the agent’s context.

The “OpenClaw memory: vector path” described in the handout can be understood in this way. Memory notes are chunked and embedded, then stored in a vector index. A new question is converted into a query embedding, nearest chunks are retrieved, and those chunks are placed into the agent context.

11 Database, SQL, DuckDB, Postgres, and S3

Dominic then moved from text data to structured data. He noted that students may often use Excel in school, but in companies and financial institutions, data is usually not stored only in Excel. It is often stored in databases such as PostgreSQL, DuckDB, or other SQL databases.

Dominic explained that SQL stands for Structured Query Language, which is used to query and manipulate databases. For example: 

SELECT * FROM trades LIMIT 10;

This command can be used to view the first ten rows of a trades table. The handout also mentioned that DuckDB can query local data, connect to Postgres through a connection string, and read parquet files stored on S3.

Dominic pointed out that if an AI agent can connect to a database, it can do much more than answer text-based questions. For example, a user could ask, “What is the monthly sales trend this year?”, “Which product has the highest revenue?”, “Please compare sales performance across regions,” or “Please draw a revenue line chart.” If the agent knows the database schema and has an SQL query tool, it can generate SQL, query the data, summarize the results, and even call a charting tool to produce graphs.

12 Assistant UI Demo: Building a Chatbot Interface

Different templates can also be selected, such as a minimal template or an example using AI SDK v7.

Today, open-source frameworks allow developers to create prototypes very quickly.

13 Connecting the Chatbot to an LLM Provider

After building the Assistant UI interface, Dominic demonstrated how to connect it to an LLM provider. He explained that many templates use the OpenAI API by default, but in different regions or usage environments, developers may choose other providers. In the demonstration, he attempted to replace the OpenAI connection with Kimi API.

Dominic explained that this involves an API key and an endpoint. An API key can be understood as the key to using the service, while an endpoint is the interface through which the app communicates with the model service. The app sends user messages, system prompts, tool definitions, and other information to the LLM provider, and the model returns a response.

In the live demonstration, Dominic asked a coding agent to help modify the codebase so that the app would use Kimi instead of OpenAI. This showed how modern coding workflows are changing. In the past, developers might have searched documentation, Google, or Stack Overflow. Now, a coding agent can read the codebase, modify files, install packages, and handle errors.

However, Dominic reminded students that this is not magic. API keys may be configured incorrectly, environment variables may be missing, endpoints may be wrong, and packages may not be installed. Even with AI assistance, developers still need to understand the basic logic of debugging.

14 Adding a Web Search Tool: From LLM to Agent

Dominic then demonstrated how to turn a chatbot into something closer to an agent. At first, the chatbot is only an LLM. It can answer general questions based on its training data. However, if a user asks for the latest stock price of SK Hynix, the model may not know because it does not have live market data.

Therefore, Dominic showed how to add a web search tool. When the agent needs updated information, it can trigger a tool call, search the web, and answer based on the results.

Dominic emphasized that if AI cannot answer a question, it does not necessarily mean that the model is not intelligent. It may simply lack the right tool. Similarly, a person who cannot access the internet, use a database, or use a calculator may not be able to answer a question about the latest price or perform precise calculations, even if they are very capable.

In the demonstration, after the web search tool was added, the agent began to perform tool calls. However, tool calls can also fail. The search result may be empty, the API response format may not match expectations, or the tool may not be properly configured. Dominic explained that these are real problems developers encounter when building AI agents.

15 Coding Agent and Pair Coding

Dominic also demonstrated the use of a coding agent. He explained that the main difference between a coding agent and a normal chatbot is that the coding agent can use development tools. It can read the codebase, search files, edit code, run terminal commands, install npm packages, read compiler errors, and then make further corrections based on those errors.

Dominic compared this workflow to pair coding. Traditional pair programming involves two people working together: one writes code while the other observes and checks. A coding agent can, in some sense, serve as a developer’s pair programmer by helping to try, modify, check, and debug code.

At the same time, Dominic reminded students that coding fundamentals remain important. Developers still need to understand project structure, how the frontend and backend communicate, what API routes are, how package.json works, how to read TypeScript errors, what localhost means, how environment variables are set, and why a database connection might fail. AI can speed up the process, but humans still need to understand the system.

16 Building a Company Sales Database with Postgres and Docker

Dominic then demonstrated an example closer to a real business application: building a company sales database that an agent could query. In the demonstration, the company database included tables such as products, sales, and sale_items.

Dominic explained that such data can be stored in a PostgreSQL database. To quickly create a database environment on a local machine, he used Docker. Docker can place the database and its runtime environment inside a container, making it easier to reproduce the same environment on different computers.

Dominic explained that a Docker image contains the environment needed to run a service. When a developer runs the image, it creates a container. The container can be thought of as an isolated mini-environment that can run Postgres, a web server, or another service.

In the demonstration, Dominic ran Postgres locally on a port such as 5433, allowing the app or agent to connect through a connection string. He emphasized that useful AI agents often need to connect to real data sources, such as company databases, internal documents, workflow systems, or financial models, instead of merely answering general knowledge questions.

17 Giving the Agent Database Schema as Context

Dominic then pointed out that if an agent is to query a database, it needs more than an SQL tool. It also needs to know the schema. A schema describes the structure of a database, including what tables exist, what columns they contain, and how those tables are related.

For example, a products table may contain product_id, name, sku, and category. A sales table may contain sale_id, sold_at, and customer_id. A sale_items table may contain sale_id, product_id, quantity, and unit_price.

Dominic explained that if the agent does not know the schema, it may query the wrong table or column even if it has a database tool. Therefore, in the demonstration, he added the schema to the system prompt or agent context so that the agent would know the available data structure whenever it answered a question.

Dominic described this as context engineering. Compared with simply writing one prompt, AI agents need complete, structured, and accurate context, including background information, tool descriptions, database structure, usage limitations, and answer rules.

18 Querying Sales Data and Generating Charts

Once the agent had both a database tool and schema context, Dominic demonstrated how it could answer questions about sales data. For example, a user could ask the agent to summarize sales data, identify the highest-revenue product, calculate total revenue, or draw a monthly sales trend.

Dominic explained that the agent can first generate SQL, query Postgres, and then organize the results in a human-readable way. If a charting tool is added, such as a frontend charting library, the agent can also visualize the results as a line chart or bar chart.

Dominic used this example to illustrate a complete AI workflow. The user asks a natural-language question. The agent decides that it needs to query the database. It generates SQL based on the schema. The SQL tool queries Postgres. The agent receives the result. It explains the result in words. If necessary, it then calls a charting tool to display the result visually.

This demonstrated the practical value of AI in business settings. Dominic emphasized that AI is not only about “chatting.” It can integrate natural language, databases, analytical tools, and data visualization into an executable workflow.

19 System Prompt, Tool Calling, Guardrails, and Evaluation

Through the demonstration, Dominic explained the importance of the system prompt. When a user types a message into the interface, the LLM does not receive only that one message. The system usually also includes the agent’s role, available tools, tool input formats, database schema, answer rules, failure-handling instructions, and safety restrictions.

Dominic explained that these design elements are central to an AI system. Without a clear system prompt and tool instructions, even an agent with tools may use them incorrectly.

He also introduced the concept of guardrails. Guardrails are control mechanisms that prevent AI from behaving in unreasonable ways. For example, if a tool call returns an empty result, the AI should not invent an answer. If the database does not contain a certain table, it should not pretend that it found one. If the API key is missing, it should point out the configuration issue. If the answer is uncertain, it should explain the limitation.

Another important concept is evaluation. Dominic explained that AI systems must be testable. Developers need to check whether the SQL is correct, whether the chart reflects the data, whether the answer is grounded, whether the tool call is reasonable, whether the cost is too high, and whether the latency is acceptable.

22 AI in Education: Easier or Harder?

During the Q&A session, a student asked whether AI would make learning easier or harder. Dominic responded that AI makes it easier to complete tasks on the surface, but it may also widen the gap in genuine learning.

Dominic explained that if students simply ask AI to complete their homework, they may be able to submit polished answers quickly in the short term. However, if they do not truly understand the material, they may struggle in examinations, interviews, project presentations, or real work situations.

He mentioned that similar issues already appear in university courses. Some assignments allow AI use, or it is difficult to completely prevent students from using AI. By contrast, Dominic argued that if students use AI as a learning partner—to ask questions, explore reasons, compare methods, generate practice problems, check mistakes, and explain concepts—then AI can help them learn faster and more deeply.

23 Coding Fundamentals in the AI Era

However, students still need to understand how programs run, how servers start, how the frontend connects to the backend, what API endpoints are, how databases are queried, how authentication works, how package dependencies are managed, how deployment is done, and how error messages should be read.

26 The Real Future: LLM + Tools + Context + Workflow

In his summary, Dominic explained that the future of AI is not about a single model alone. Instead, it is about:

\[\text{LLM}+\text{Tools}+\text{Context}+\text{Workflow}+\text{Evaluation}\]

If there is only an LLM, it is simply a model that can speak. If tools are added, it can perform actions. If context is added, it understands the environment in which it is acting. If workflow is added, it can complete tasks continuously. If evaluation is added, it has a chance of becoming reliable.

Dominic believed that for students, the most important thing is not to chase every new tool, but to build fundamental capabilities. These include mathematics, statistics, financial knowledge, computational thinking, AI literacy, data literacy, problem formulation, critical thinking, and communication.

If it has to analyze market news, it needs a web search tool or internal research tool. If it has to perform financial modelling, it needs the appropriate modelling tools.

Dominic again emphasized the importance of context engineering. He explained that users cannot simply give AI a short question and expect it to perform correctly in every situation. Users need to provide sufficient background, a clear task, appropriate tools, and error-handling mechanisms.

Dominic also mentioned guardrails and evaluation. When an agent uses tools, it needs to know when a result is empty, when it should stop, and when it should admit that it cannot find an answer, rather than blindly retrying. This is especially important in enterprise systems, because incorrect answers may lead to incorrect decisions.

Finally, Dominic responded to students’ interest in different AI tools and platforms. He explained that different platforms have different strengths. Some are better for coding, some are better for Chinese-language use, some are more suitable for enterprise deployment, and some are better for personal learning. For students, the most important point is to understand the principles behind the tools, not merely to memorize tool names.
